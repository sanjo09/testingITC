MENTAL HEALTH & SUPPORTYour mental health matters, every day.Understand why it matters, find small ways to care for yourself, and learn how to reach out for support.
UNDERSTANDING THE IMPORTANCEMental health is part of everyday health.It includes our emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It influences how we think, feel, connect with others, and respond to life.
01 / HOW YOU FEELUnderstanding yourselfRecognizing your feelings and needs can help you respond to difficult moments with more care.
02 / HOW YOU CONNECTBuilding connectionMental well-being shapes how we communicate, build relationships, and feel connected to others.
03 / HOW YOU COPEFacing everyday lifeCaring for your mental health can support focus, decision-making, and coping with everyday stress.
Mental health matters at every age—not only when things feel difficult.
SMALL WAYS TO CARECare doesn’t have to be complicated.You don’t need a perfect routine. Start with one small thing that feels manageable today.
Make room for restPause when you can. Regular sleep, meals, and gentle movement can support your well-being.
Stay connectedSpend time with people who help you feel heard. A small check-in can be a starting point.
Notice what you needPay attention to changes in your mood, energy, or focus. Give yourself permission to ask for help.
GENERAL HELP & SUPPORTYou don’t have to work through it alone.Support can begin with a conversation. Choose a starting point that feels comfortable for you.
01 / A FAMILIAR PERSONSomeone you trustA friend, family member, or trusted adult can listen and help you take the next step. You can ask for company without sharing every detail.You can say: “Could you listen for a bit?”
02 / YOUR EVERYDAY SPACESSchool or workplace supportA school counselor, teacher, student support team, or workplace support service may help you explore what support is available.Ask what is available and how privacy works.
03 / PROFESSIONAL GUIDANCEA counselor or health professionalA qualified mental health professional or healthcare provider can help you understand what you’re experiencing and discuss options.You don’t need a diagnosis to ask for help.
NOT SURE WHAT TO SAY?“I haven’t been feeling like myself lately. Could we talk? I don’t need you to fix it—I just need someone to listen.”You can say it aloud, send a message, or write it down. Share only what you’re ready to share.
When is it time to reach out?Consider extra support when difficult feelings persist, become intense, or affect your sleep, studies, work, or relationships.You don’t have to wait until things feel unbearable.
If you feel unsafeIf you might hurt yourself or someone else, seek immediate help from emergency services and tell a trusted person who can stay with you.
This page provides general information, not a diagnosis, therapy, or emergency care. Everyday self-care can complement—but does not replace—professional support.
A LITTLE REASSURANCEAsking for support is a way of caring for yourself. You are worth that care.Start small. Take your time. You don’t have to do it all alone.
